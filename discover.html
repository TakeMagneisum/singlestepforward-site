What might be worth looking for?

These examples describe common forms of assistance—not promises that a particular program exists or that anyone qualifies. Confirm details directly.

More than benefit programs

Help can take many forms.

It might be a patient advocate who reviews a medical bill, a clinic with income-based pricing, an ombudsman who explains your rights, a payment plan, a transportation coordinator, a nonprofit navigator, or simply someone who knows which office to call next.

A useful next step

Sometimes the useful step is not receiving money. It is learning that you have another option, a question you can ask, or a person who can help you avoid an unnecessary expense.
