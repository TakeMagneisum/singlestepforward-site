Technology & judgment

Use AI with purpose

AI tools can help explain, draft, translate, and organize information. A fluent or confident answer still needs checking when the stakes matter.

First-pass educational guide · November 15, 2026

A five-part safety habit

  1. Define the task. Say what you need and what decision the answer will support.
  2. Minimize what you share. Avoid passwords, verification codes, identity records, private case details, and confidential third-party information.
  3. Request an explanation. Ask what is known, assumed, and uncertain.
  4. Verify the deciding fact. Open the actual official source. Check its date, service area, and whether it supports the claim.
  5. Act deliberately. Recheck the address, recipient, and consequences before sending, paying, or publishing.

Common ways AI can mislead

  • Invented facts and citations: A plausible-looking source or link may not exist, may be outdated, or may not support the statement.
  • Voice and image imitation: Familiar-sounding audio or realistic visuals do not prove a person said or did something.
  • Prompt injection: A webpage, email, or retrieved file may contain instructions meant to redirect an AI assistant. Treat external content as untrusted evidence, not authority.
  • Overreliance: For important decisions, verify with the agency or qualified professional that can actually make the decision.

A quick example

An AI assistant says that a local rental program accepts applicants today. First open the program’s official website, check its latest notice, then contact the agency using its published details. A generated answer is not confirmation of current funding or eligibility.
