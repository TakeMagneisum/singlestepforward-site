Family safety

A family verification plan

A convincing voice, video, text, or caller ID is not reliable proof of identity—especially when someone asks for urgent money or secrecy.

First-pass educational guide · November 15, 2026

Set a plan before anyone needs it

  1. Agree privately on a short phrase or challenge that strangers would not easily know. Avoid birthdays, names, or public social-media facts.
  2. Decide which existing phone numbers or trusted contacts you can use to confirm a real emergency.
  3. Tell family members that an urgent request involving money, secrecy, or identity information always gets independently verified.
  4. If the phrase becomes known to others, replace it. Do not put it into websites, public documents, or SSF emails.

If an urgent call seems to be from family

  1. Do not send money, login codes, gift cards, cryptocurrency, or personal records during the call.
  2. End the conversation and call your family member directly using a number you already trust.
  3. If you cannot reach them, check with another trusted family member or known contact.
  4. If there is an independently confirmed emergency, act through appropriate official or local services.
Important: The family phrase is only an additional check. It can be overheard, forgotten, or pressured out of someone. An incorrect answer does not prove someone is safe or unsafe; independent contact remains the main safeguard.
